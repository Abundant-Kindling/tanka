## 1. The estate and the current position

The Trust operates eleven sites across three counties: roughly 240 acres of grounds, 1.1 million square feet of hard landscaping and external hardstanding, and external building fabric across 78 individual structures ranging from Victorian brick to 2019 modular. Responsibility currently sits with individual site bursars, each holding local contractor arrangements of varying age and formality.

The Trust's stated objectives were consistency of standard across sites, visibility of spend, and a defensible compliance position on external safety inspections.

Discovery visits were conducted at all eleven sites between 4 and 22 September 2026. Three findings shaped this proposal:

1. **Grounds standards vary widely between sites,** driven less by budget than by each bursar's contractor relationships and available time.
2. **No site holds a complete record of external fabric inspection,** and four sites could not produce evidence of any inspection within the past two years.
3. **Aggregate spend is materially higher than the Trust believes,** because a portion sits in site-level operational budgets rather than the central facilities line.

## 2. Commercial in full

Priced as an annual managed fee, per site, with a single mobilisation charge.

| Component | Annual fee (excl. VAT) |
|---|---|

Average per site, £48,473 per annum. Mobilisation is a one-off £38,500 excluding VAT, invoiced on contract award.

The minor repair threshold is £750 per instance, with an annual aggregate cap of £60,000. Repairs above the threshold or beyond the cap are quoted individually.

Fees are fixed for the first 24 months, then reviewed annually against CPIH capped at 4 per cent.

The contract term is three years with a break at 24 months on six months' notice.

## 3. What we need from the Trust

To mobilise we require:

- The eleven site plans with boundaries and asset positions marked
- Existing contractor agreements and their notice periods
- The last two years of grounds and external fabric spend by site
- Existing inspection records where held
- A nominated Trust contact, and a nominated contact at each site
- Confirmation of term dates and any access restrictions for the coming academic year

Where records are incomplete we establish the baseline during the condition survey at no additional cost.

## 4. The service model

**Grounds maintenance.** A single specification across all eleven sites, seasonally scheduled: grass and meadow management, hedge and tree work within reach, borders, sports pitch preparation and line marking, hard surface cleaning, gritting and snow clearance, and drainage clearance.

**External fabric inspection and minor repair.** An annual condition inspection of all 78 structures against a consistent scoring framework, with a photographic record and a prioritised defect register. Minor repairs within the threshold at section 2 are carried out without further authorisation; anything above it is quoted separately.

**Compliance and reporting.** A single quarterly report covering standards achieved, spend by site, the defect register and its movement, and any statutory inspection due or overdue. Site bursars retain visibility of their own site through the same reporting.

## 5. Standards and inspection framework

Grounds standards are set against BS 7370 parts 1 to 4.

External fabric inspection follows the Trust's own risk categories, mapped to a four-point condition score: Good, Fair, Poor, Failed. Any structure scoring Failed triggers notification to the Trust within 24 hours and a remedial proposal within five working days.

Playground and external play equipment inspection is carried out to BS EN 1176 by an RPII-certificated inspector, annually, with quarterly visual checks by the grounds team.

## 6. Mobilisation

Ten weeks from contract award.

| Weeks | Activity |
|---|---|
| 1 to 3 | Contractor novation and TUPE consultation where applicable. Site inductions. Access and safeguarding clearance for all operatives |
| 3 to 6 | Baseline condition survey of all 78 structures. Defect register established |
| 6 to 9 | First full grounds cycle at each site. Reporting rhythm established |
| 10 | First quarterly report and formal review with the Trust |

**Safeguarding clearance is the critical path.** Enhanced DBS checks for all operatives working on site during term time must be complete before mobilisation begins, and current processing times are four to six weeks. This is what sets the 15 November acceptance date in the summary.

## 7. Assumptions and exclusions

**Assumed access.** School holidays for works requiring plant or generating noise. Term-time access restricted to before 08:00 and after 16:30 unless otherwise agreed.

**Excluded from the programme:**

- Tree work requiring a climbing arborist, or above 6 metres. Quoted per instance
- Roof-level work above 3 metres
- Anything requiring scaffolding or a mobile elevated work platform
- Internal fabric of any kind
- Sports hall and swimming pool plant
- Asbestos survey, management or removal
- Statutory electrical or gas inspection

Our RPII-certificated inspection capability is in-house rather than subcontracted, which is unusual at this scale and is what makes the compliance position the Trust asked for defensible.

Retention across education clients is 94 per cent over the past five years. References from two trusts of similar composition are available on request.

**Nothing was cut.** Every figure, date, standard, exclusion, threshold and requirement in the source appears in the output: £533,200, £38,500, £48,473, £412,800, £96,400, £24,000, £750, £60,000, 4 per cent CPIH, 15 November 2026, 4 to 22 September 2026, BS 7370, BS EN 1176, RPII, 11 sites, 78 structures, 240 acres, 1.1 million square feet, 34 sites, 4 trusts, 19 years, 94 per cent, all seven exclusions, both access windows, the 24-hour and five-day triggers, and the 24-month break on six months' notice.

**What moved, and why**

- **The fee came forward from section 9 to line one.** It was the last substantive thing in the document and it is the first thing any reader wants
- **Commercial and requirements moved ahead of service mechanics.** A reader returning to a signed proposal goes looking for a number or an obligation, not a description of hedge cutting
- **The 15 November date was given its reason.** In the source it arrived in the final paragraph with the DBS explanation three sections earlier. Now they sit together in layer 2, and the date reads as a constraint rather than a sales tactic
- **The four uninspected sites were promoted into layer 2.** In the source this sat mid-paragraph in section 2 as one of three discovery findings. It is a live compliance exposure for the Trust whatever they decide about this proposal, so it belongs where a decision-maker will see it
- **"Why Kestrel" moved down.** Credentials matter to a reader already interested. They do not help anyone decide whether to keep reading

**What was tightened, not cut:** "Kestrel Facilities Group is pleased to present this proposal for" and "We are ready to proceed as soon as the Trust is comfortable with the scope and pricing" both went. Section 5's mobilisation paragraph and section 6's requirements paragraph became a table and a list, which is where the 108 words came back from.
