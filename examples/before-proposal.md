# Managed Grounds and External Fabric Programme

**Prepared for Thornbury Independent Schools Trust**
**Prepared by Kestrel Facilities Group**
**Version 2, October 2026**

## 1. Introduction

Kestrel Facilities Group is pleased to present this proposal for a managed grounds and external fabric programme across the Thornbury Independent Schools Trust estate.

The Trust operates eleven sites across three counties, comprising roughly 240 acres of grounds, 1.1 million square feet of hard landscaping and external hardstanding, and external building fabric across 78 individual structures ranging from Victorian brick to 2019 modular. Responsibility for maintaining this estate currently sits with individual site bursars, each holding local contractor arrangements of varying age and formality.

The Trust's stated objectives in commissioning this proposal were consistency of standard across sites, visibility of spend, and a defensible compliance position on external safety inspections. This proposal addresses each.

## 2. Our understanding of the current position

Discovery visits were conducted at all eleven sites between 4 and 22 September 2026.

Three findings shaped this proposal. First, grounds standards vary widely between sites, driven less by budget than by the individual bursar's contractor relationships and available time. Second, no site holds a complete record of external fabric inspection, and four sites could not produce evidence of any inspection within the past two years. Third, aggregate spend across the eleven sites is materially higher than the Trust believes, because a portion sits in site-level operational budgets rather than the central facilities line.

## 3. Proposed service model

The programme has three components.

**Grounds maintenance.** A single specification applied across all eleven sites, seasonally scheduled, covering grass and meadow management, hedge and tree work within reach, borders, sports pitch preparation and line marking, hard surface cleaning, gritting and snow clearance, and drainage clearance.

**External fabric inspection and minor repair.** An annual condition inspection of all 78 structures against a consistent scoring framework, with a photographic record and a prioritised defect register. Minor repairs within a defined threshold are carried out under the programme without further authorisation; anything above the threshold is quoted separately.

**Compliance and reporting.** A single quarterly report to the Trust covering standards achieved, spend by site, the defect register and its movement, and any statutory inspection due or overdue. Site bursars retain visibility of their own site through the same reporting.

## 4. Standards and inspection framework

Grounds standards are set against BS 7370 parts 1 to 4. External fabric inspection follows the Trust's own risk categories, mapped to a four-point condition score: Good, Fair, Poor, Failed. Any structure scoring Failed triggers notification to the Trust within 24 hours and a remedial proposal within five working days.

Playground and external play equipment inspection is carried out to BS EN 1176 by an RPII-certificated inspector, annually, with quarterly visual checks by the grounds team.

## 5. Mobilisation

Mobilisation runs over ten weeks from contract award.

Weeks 1 to 3 cover contractor novation and TUPE consultation where applicable, site inductions, and access and safeguarding clearance for all operatives. Weeks 3 to 6 cover the baseline condition survey of all 78 structures and the establishment of the defect register. Weeks 6 to 9 cover the first full grounds cycle at each site and the establishment of the reporting rhythm. Week 10 is the first quarterly report and a formal review with the Trust.

Safeguarding clearance is the critical path. Enhanced DBS checks for all operatives working on site during term time must be complete before mobilisation begins, and current processing times are running at four to six weeks.

## 6. What we need from the Trust

To mobilise we require: the eleven site plans with boundaries and asset positions marked; existing contractor agreements and their notice periods; the last two years of grounds and external fabric spend by site; existing inspection records where held; a nominated Trust contact and a nominated contact at each site; and confirmation of term dates and any access restrictions for the coming academic year.

Where records are incomplete we will establish the baseline during the condition survey at no additional cost.

## 7. Assumptions and exclusions

The programme assumes access during school holidays for works requiring plant or generating noise, and term-time access restricted to before 08:00 and after 16:30 unless otherwise agreed.

Excluded from the programme: tree work requiring a climbing arborist or above 6 metres, which is quoted per instance; roof-level work above 3 metres; anything requiring scaffolding or a mobile elevated work platform; internal fabric of any kind; sports hall and swimming pool plant; asbestos survey, management or removal; and statutory electrical or gas inspection.

## 8. Why Kestrel

Kestrel has held managed grounds contracts in the independent education sector for nineteen years. We currently maintain 34 sites across four trusts, including two of comparable scale to Thornbury. Our RPII-certificated inspection capability is in-house rather than subcontracted, which is unusual at this scale and matters for the compliance position the Trust has asked for.

Our retention rate across education clients is 94 per cent over the past five years, and we can provide references from two trusts of similar composition on request.
