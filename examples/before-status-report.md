**Subject:** WMS Rollout, Programme Update, October
**To:** Nordvale Logistics Steering Group
**From:** Priya Raghavan, Programme Lead

Hi everyone,

I hope this finds you well, and thanks again for making time at short notice last month. Apologies for the length of this one.

I am writing to provide the steering group with an update on the current status of the warehouse management system rollout, to set out where things stand across each of our workstreams, and to raise a matter that has arisen which will need a decision from the group.

This update covers programme status, the position at each site, our current blockers, budget, and next steps. Before we begin, some context on how we got here.

## Background

As the group will recall, the WMS programme was approved in February following the operational review, which identified inventory accuracy as the single largest driver of our fulfilment error rate. The business case set a target of reducing picking errors by 40 per cent within twelve months of full deployment. Since then the team has worked steadily through the discovery, configuration and pilot phases, and I am pleased to report that considerable progress has been made across the board.

## Site by site

**Redbourne.** The pilot site went live in August and has now been running for nine weeks. Picking accuracy has improved from 94.1 per cent to 98.6 per cent. Staff feedback has been broadly positive.

**Calderwood.** Configuration is complete and user acceptance testing is scheduled.

**Ashgrove.** Ashgrove remains on track and there is nothing further to report on Ashgrove at this stage.

**Hetherington.** Hetherington also remains on track with no material change since the last update, and we continue to monitor progress there closely.

**Marbury.** No change.

## Blockers

We have encountered a number of blockers which I should bring to the group's attention:

1. The Calderwood UAT cannot begin until the interface to the finance ledger is signed off by Finance, and that sign-off has been outstanding for three weeks.
2. Two of the four scanner models specified in the original configuration have been discontinued by the manufacturer, so we need a decision on the replacement model.
3. We are carrying a vacancy in the integration team which has been open since July.

If these blockers are not resolved, we will not be able to proceed to the next phase of the programme on the current timeline. It is important to note that delays at this stage tend to compound.

## Budget

The programme is currently forecasting an overspend. The forecast position is £84,000 above the approved budget of £610,000. This is driven principally by the scanner replacement, which carries a higher unit cost than the discontinued model, and by contractor cover for the vacant integration role.

I should say that the overspend is not ideal, although it may be that the final position lands somewhat better than the current forecast suggests once we have firmer pricing.

## A note on the scanner decision

I want to be clear that the decision to specify the original scanner model was the right one at the time it was made. The model was current, it was competitively priced, and it had been validated in the discovery phase. Nobody could reasonably have anticipated that it would be withdrawn mid-programme. I have reviewed the original selection process and I am satisfied that it was sound. The alternative would have been to defer the hardware decision until later in the programme, but that would have delayed the pilot, and on balance I remain confident that we made the correct call with the information available.

## Out of scope

For completeness, I should note that this update does not cover the transport management system, which sits under a separate programme and a separate steering group. Nor does it cover the depot consolidation work, the fleet telematics refresh, or the changes to the returns process, all of which are being tracked elsewhere. Questions on any of those are best directed to the relevant programme leads rather than to me.

## Summary of the above

To recap, the programme has made good progress, with Redbourne live and performing well and the remaining sites broadly on track. We have three blockers, the most pressing of which is the Finance sign-off. We are forecasting an overspend of £84,000 against the approved budget, driven by the scanner replacement and contractor cover.

## Next steps

The group is asked to approve the £84,000 budget increase, and to confirm which scanner model we should proceed with, so that Calderwood can be brought into UAT without further slippage. We would need both by 14 November to hold the current go-live date.

Happy to discuss any of the above at your convenience, and do let me know if you would like me to expand on any section.
